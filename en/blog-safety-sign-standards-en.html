Safety Sign Standards Required by Thai Law: What Business Owners Need to Know

Published Jun 15, 2026 6 min read CS.SIGN Engineering Team
Safety Sign Standards Under Thai Law

Many organizations invest heavily in machinery, fire-suppression systems, and personal protective equipment, but overlook safety signage — even though Thai law requires many types of workplaces to install signs that meet specific standards, and signage is one of the items inspectors check most often during factory or workplace visits. This article summarizes the key laws and standards involved, to give business owners the full picture before commissioning signage.

1. The core laws governing safety signage

The Occupational Safety, Health and Environment Act is the umbrella law requiring employers to provide a safe working environment, which includes posting hazard warning signs, mandatory signs, and signs marking areas where protective equipment must be worn. Ministerial regulations and notifications issued by the Department of Labour Protection and Welfare under this Act then set out further details specific to each type of business.

Separately, building control law requires public buildings and high-rise buildings to display fire-escape signage, assembly-point signs, and signage marking the location of fire-fighting equipment, in the positions and sizes it specifies. This is a distinct set of requirements from factory safety signage, so organizations that operate both office buildings and production facilities need to consider both standards together.

2. TIS — Thailand's safety sign standard

TIS is the Thai Industrial Standard that defines the shape, color, and symbols used on safety signage specifically for use in Thailand. It covers warning signs (yellow background), prohibition signs (white background, red border), mandatory signs (blue background), and safe-condition signs (green background). Using colors or symbols that don't match this standard can cause a sign to fail inspection even when its message is otherwise clear.

3. International safety symbols for export-oriented businesses

For factories with overseas customers, that are audited by an overseas parent company, or that need international certification, safety signage often needs to use pictograms that follow a single global standard, so that staff and foreign visitors can understand the meaning without relying on language. In practice, many organizations produce signage that combines TIS standards with international symbols on a single sign, so it satisfies both domestic inspection and international standards.

A word of caution: Signage from manufacturers without standards certification may use colors and dimensions that don't match legal requirements, which can cause a business to fail inspection. Choose a manufacturer that can produce a TIS certificate on request, every time.

4. Locations where the law typically requires signage

AreaSign type typically required
Entrances to hazardous areas (machinery, high voltage)Hazard warning signs, mandatory PPE signs
Fire-escape routes and stairwellsFire-exit signs, directional signs
Assembly pointsStandard-size assembly-point signs
Chemical / flammable material storage areasHazardous-substance warning signs, no-smoking signs
Parking areas and internal factory roadsTraffic signs, speed-limit signs

5. Guidance for procurement and safety teams

  • Survey the site together with the sign manufacturer to identify every location that legally requires signage, so nothing is missed
  • Request standards-certification documents for each sign as supporting records for audits
  • Check that existing signage is still clearly legible and hasn't faded, especially outdoor signs exposed to continuous sun and rain
  • Plan signage upgrades ahead of your annual inspection date, to allow enough time for production and installation

Investing in signage that meets the required standards isn't just about passing inspection — it's about reducing the real risk of accidents to your employees and visitors.
